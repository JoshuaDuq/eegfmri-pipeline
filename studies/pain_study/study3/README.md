# Materials and Methods

## Research Objectives
This study systematically interpreted the primary predictive model from Study 1 by deriving spatially resolved EEG source-power covariance patterns associated with its trial-wise predictions. The primary interpretive target was fixed before source-map inspection: the non-transductive ElasticNet model trained on Level 2 stimulus- and acquisition-controlled NPS expression from the alpha+beta spectral-power preset in Study 1. This hierarchy does not demote SIIPS1 as a Study 1 co-primary prediction target; it narrows the source-interpretation target to NPS because the source claim specifically concerns nociceptive fMRI signature expression, whereas SIIPS1 indexes a related but distinct stimulus-intensity-independent construct. SIIPS1, Level 1 raw expression, Level 3 rating-residualized sensitivity targets, Ridge regression, Random Forest, and BandTemporalRegressor were evaluated only as sensitivity or exploratory analyses.

Study 3 was gated on Study 1 before any source-map inspection. Confirmatory source interpretation was permitted only if the exact Study 1 Level 2 NPS ElasticNet alpha+beta model showed positive mean LOSO $R^2$, survived Holm correction in the Study 1 confirmatory family, passed temporal negative controls, and retained its sign and inferential status under the prespecified HRF/timing, baseline, and artifact robustness analyses. If this gate failed, all Study 3 maps and behavioral associations were reported as exploratory. This design aimed to test the fixed alpha+beta full-plateau source-space projection associated with EEG-based prediction of stimulus- and acquisition-controlled fMRI pain-signature expression, producing a candidate pain-relevant EEG source-power covariance pattern only if the internal cross-validated validation criteria below were satisfied. Exploratory source-discovery maps across additional bands or plateau subwindows were reported separately and could not define the confirmatory behavioral-validation pattern. The Haufe et al. (2014) forward-backward duality formally applies to the primary ElasticNet model. Random Forest and BandTemporalRegressor source-space maps are reported as prediction-covariance association maps without formal forward-model guarantees.

## Model Ingestion and Out-of-Sample Prediction
To prevent circular logic and interpretation of overfitted noise, all interpretive analyses were performed strictly on out-of-sample data. Following the LOSO framework from Study 1, the finalized predictive architectures were frozen before source analysis. The held-out test-set EEG data ($X_{test}$) for each subject was fed through the frozen primary model to generate a single scalar prediction per trial ($\hat{y}_{test}$) representing predicted Level 2 stimulus- and acquisition-controlled NPS expression. No Study 3 source-map statistic, anatomical result, spatial-comparison result, or behavioral-validation result was used to select Study 1 model hyperparameters, residualization level, feature family, frequency band, or time window.

## Prediction-Associated Sensor-Level Topographies
In multivariate decoding, predictive algorithms function as backward models mapping brain data ($X$) to a target variable ($y$). As demonstrated by Haufe et al. (2014), the raw internal weights of backward models can highlight noise-canceling sensors rather than true signal sources. To obtain interpretable maps, the formal Haufe transformation was used where its assumptions applied and empirical prediction-covariance maps where they did not. Because the models utilized distinct input architectures, covariance computations were segregated and resulting maps named according to their theoretical guarantees.

For the primary linear model (ElasticNet), the Haufe transformation is defined as:

$$A_{Haufe} = \Sigma_{X,train} \, W$$

where $\Sigma_{X,train}$ is the training-fold data covariance matrix after the exact Study 1 preprocessing transform and $W$ is the fitted backward-model weight vector. Because Study 1 trains ElasticNet on standardized features and Yeo-Johnson transformed targets, the formal Haufe pattern was interpreted in transformed-target units per standardized-feature unit. Held-out empirical covariance maps used transformed-scale out-of-sample predictions for comparability with the formal pattern. Inverse-transformed active-scale predictions were used only for performance reporting and behavioral calibration, not for replacing the formal Haufe map. The Haufe pattern was computed separately within each outer fold using training-fold covariance only.

For nonlinear models (Random Forest and BandTemporalRegressor), $\mathrm{Cov}(X, \hat{y})$ does not carry the formal guarantees of the Haufe transformation. A feature can covary with $\hat{y}$ because it is correlated with another feature the model actually used, and nonlinear interactions can be missed by simple covariance. These maps were therefore designated prediction-covariance association maps, indicating which sensor-level features are linearly associated with the model's output without claims of causal feature attribution:

$$A_{assoc} \propto \mathrm{Cov}(X_{features},\; \hat{y}_{test})$$

Only spectral power features were subjected to source-space follow-up, and source-space power was recomputed from inverse-projected voltage time series rather than from projected sensor-level power maps. Projecting nonlinear complexity metrics (e.g., permutation entropy, fractal dimension) through a lead-field matrix is physically invalid. Sensor-level topographies for complexity features are reported separately.

## Artifact Sensitivity Analysis
Because machine learning algorithms can inadvertently exploit non-neural biological noise to predict task variables, sensor-level maps were compared against artifact topographies derived from independent calibration data or from components explicitly labeled as ocular, cardiac, scanner, or electromyographic artifacts during preprocessing. Template correlations were treated as artifact flags, not correction procedures. Primary maps were not orthogonalized against artifact templates because orthogonalization can remove neural signal when neural and artifact topographies spatially overlap.

A map was labeled artifact-contaminated when its absolute spatial correlation exceeded $r = 0.8$ with any prespecified artifact template or when its expression covaried with framewise displacement, DVARS, cardiac phase, scanner-frequency residual power, or temporal/frontal high-frequency electromyographic power after Holm correction. Artifact-contaminated maps were excluded from confirmatory interpretation and reported only as robustness failures.

## Spatio-Temporal Prediction-Covariance Mapping for the Deep Network
While static covariance mapping interprets the classical machine learning models, the BandTemporalRegressor processes continuous epochs capturing temporal dynamics. Because the covariance computation is model-agnostic—requiring only the model's final scalar prediction $\hat{y}$—it was extended across the time dimension to interpret the deep network. As a nonlinear model, the resulting maps are prediction-covariance association maps rather than formal Haufe forward patterns.

Because pain-evoked high-frequency oscillations are largely induced and non-phase-locked, computing cross-trial covariance on raw oscillatory voltage would result in destructive phase cancellation. The spatio-temporal prediction-covariance map was therefore computed on instantaneous power envelopes ($E_{test}(t)$) continuously across the stimulation window:

$$A_{assoc}(t) \propto \mathrm{Cov}(E_{test}(t),\; \hat{y}_{test})$$

This produced a map of shape bands $\times$ channels $\times$ time, identifying the time periods at which prediction-associated covariance is maximal during thermal stimulation. Because this analysis relies on the deep network, which is exploratory given subject-level sample size (see Study 1), the temporal maps are interpreted as exploratory characterizations. Time-resolved analyses were evaluated within predefined windows (early, mid, and late plateau) to control temporal multiplicity.

## Anatomical Source Projection
To project prediction-associated EEG variance into source space, source-space analysis was performed using a pipeline that respects linearity constraints of the electromagnetic forward model. These maps were interpreted as spatially smoothed source-space covariance projections, not as causal localization of cortical origins. Inverse modeling was applied to band-passed voltage time series rather than to derived sensor-level power or covariance topographies. Projecting sensor-level power maps through a lead-field matrix is invalid because power is a nonlinear, nonnegative summary of voltage that does not represent an electric field generated by cortical current dipoles.

A fixed inverse operator was estimated from the forward model and baseline noise covariance, then applied to band-passed sensor-level voltage time series. The sLORETA inverse operator (Pascual-Marqui, 2002) was used, with the forward solution derived from FreeSurfer subject-specific head models and boundary element method solutions. The primary noise covariance matrix was empirically derived from the clean pre-stimulus baseline ($-5.0$ to $-0.01$ s), matching the Study 1 time-frequency baseline. Covariance rank was estimated with the MNE rank estimator after bad-channel interpolation and average reference projection; the estimated rank, eigenvalue spectrum, condition number, and shrinkage parameter were logged per subject and baseline window. A covariance window failed source analysis when it contained fewer clean samples than twice the estimated sensor rank, had nonfinite eigenvalues, had effective rank $< 0.5$ of the estimated sensor rank, or required shrinkage strong enough to change the retained-rank estimate. Because the primary baseline may contain cue-locked expectancy activity, source localization was repeated using the immediate pre-stimulus covariance window ($-0.2$ to $-0.01$ s) and the early pre-cue covariance window ($-7.0$ to $-5.5$ s, when event timing confirmed it preceded cues). The immediate-window sensitivity was interpreted only when it passed the same covariance-rank QC. A source-space result was considered baseline-stable only when the primary spatial pattern, cluster polarity, and behavioral-validation inference were preserved under the immediate-baseline covariance sensitivity.

The inverse operator is not frequency-band-specific: it is computed once from broadband data and applied identically to voltage time series filtered into each canonical frequency band. Instantaneous power envelopes were subsequently computed from estimated source time series using the Hilbert transform. Prediction-associated source-power covariance was then computed as $\mathrm{Cov}(\text{source power},\; \hat{y}_{test})$ directly in source space.

The sLORETA regularization was configured with an assumed SNR of 3.0, corresponding to $\lambda^2 = 1/\text{SNR}^2 \approx 0.111$, using oct6 source spacing, loose orientation constraint of 0.2, and depth weighting of 0.8. Sensitivity analyses were conducted at SNR values of 1.0, 3.0, and 5.0 ($\lambda^2 = 1.0, 0.111, 0.04$) because the optimal regularization for derived covariance maps is not well-established. Source-level gamma-band findings are reported as exploratory even if statistically significant, given the compound fragility of gamma source localization from scalp EEG during simultaneous fMRI.

Point-spread and cross-talk functions were estimated before outcome-map inspection by applying the finalized forward/inverse operators to unit-amplitude cortical impulses on the fsaverage mesh. Reports included the median and interquartile range of point-spread full-width at half-maximum, cross-talk burden within and outside the cortical analysis mask, and the spatial resolution used for fMRI smoothing in the multimodal comparison. A subject-level source-covariance map was retained only when the subject had at least 20 retained plateau trials across at least two runs after artifact censoring, nonzero finite prediction variance, nonzero finite source-power variance in the tested band/window, and participation-ratio effective rank $\geq 5$ for the source-power trial matrix. Subjects failing any criterion were excluded from source-space inference for that band/window rather than being regularized into interpretability.

To enable group-level comparison, subject-specific source maps were morphed to the standard FreeSurfer fsaverage template. Source maps were normalized by their Global Field Power before group aggregation to test spatial pattern consistency rather than absolute magnitude. This normalization removes between-subject amplitude information; group maps therefore represent consistent spatial topology rather than differential predictive cortical signal strength.

## Statistical Validation

### Permutation Strategy
To isolate prediction-associated source patterns from chance variance, non-parametric permutation testing was performed on source-localized maps. Standard sign-flipping permutations were avoided because predictive algorithms deterministically map $X$ to $\hat{y} = f(X)$, yielding source-power covariance patterns with consistent spatial structure driven by the shared intrinsic covariance of human EEG ($\Sigma_X$), regardless of whether the model successfully tracked the true fMRI targets.

Instead, a test-set prediction permutation strategy was employed. For each subject, out-of-sample predictions ($\hat{y}_{test}$) were circularly shifted relative to EEG source-power trials within each run, with a minimum shift distance of 5 trials. Circular shifts rather than full randomization approximately preserve the temporal spacing of retained predictions (Winkler et al., 2014). The number of valid unique circular shifts per subject per run was logged; runs with fewer than 10 valid trials failed permutation-inference QC. For each of 5,000 permutations, subject-level covariance maps were recomputed from shifted predictions and carried through the same group-level statistic and clustering procedure as observed maps.

### Cluster-Based Inference
Group-level inference was performed using cluster-based permutation testing (Maris & Oostenveld, 2007) on the primary model (ElasticNet) only. The confirmatory map was fixed to the alpha+beta spectral-power preset averaged across the full plateau ($3.0$–$10.5$ s), matching the gated Study 1 model cell. The exchangeability unit was the subject: each subject contributed one source-power covariance map after morphing to fsaverage and applying the fixed orientation convention that positive map values indicate greater source power on trials with larger predicted Level 2 NPS expression. Vertex-wise one-sample $t$ statistics tested whether the mean covariance differed from zero. Ridge results were evaluated as sensitivity analyses without independent multiplicity correction. Random Forest and BandTemporalRegressor maps were reported as exploratory and not subjected to formal cluster inference.

Clusters were defined over fsaverage cortical surface vertices, with adjacency determined by the cortical surface geodesic neighborhood. Vertex-wise test statistics exceeding a two-tailed uncorrected cluster-forming threshold of $p < 0.01$ were aggregated into contiguous sign-preserving clusters. Positive and negative clusters were summarized separately, with cluster mass defined as the sum of vertex-wise $t$ statistics (absolute for negative clusters). Family-wise error correction used a joint two-sided maximum statistic across positive and negative clusters in the fixed alpha+beta full-plateau confirmatory map. Observed clusters were significant only if they exceeded the 95th percentile of this joint null distribution; cluster polarity was reported descriptively. Secondary source-discovery summaries spanning alpha-only, beta-only, early, mid, late, and full-plateau windows were Holm-corrected as one exploratory family and could generate hypotheses only. Full vertex $\times$ time clustering, if performed, used space-time adjacency defined by conjoining surface geodesic adjacency with temporal nearest-neighbor adjacency and was reported as exploratory.

The primary analysis used equal subject weighting. As a sensitivity analysis, fixed baseline-noise inverse-variance weights were evaluated as a reliability proxy, acknowledging that true map reliability depends on trial count, prediction variance, artifact rejection, and source-estimation uncertainty rather than baseline noise alone.

### Gamma-Band Interpretation
Given the known vulnerability of gamma-band (30.1–80 Hz) EEG during simultaneous fMRI to facial muscle activity, jaw tension, scanner gradient residuals, cardiac artifacts, and movement, gamma-band source maps were exploratory regardless of statistical significance. Gamma findings were reported with artifact metrics and censoring analyses and were not used to designate a candidate pain-relevant EEG pattern.

## Multimodal Spatial Comparison
Spatial correspondence between the derived EEG source-power covariance pattern and fMRI pain-related covariance was evaluated as a within-cohort spatial-consistency and specificity analysis. It was not treated as independent validation because the fMRI covariance target was derived from the same study cohort and from NPS-derived expression. Because the full fMRI NPS is defined in volumetric MNI152 space and includes deep subcortical structures (e.g., thalamus, periaqueductal gray) inaccessible to EEG surface source modeling, this comparison was restricted to the cortical sub-network.

Comparing the EEG map to published NPS backward-model weights (Wager et al., 2013) would create a theoretical asymmetry: the EEG map is a forward-pattern or covariance estimate while NPS weights are raw backward-model weights containing noise-canceling structure (Haufe et al., 2014). To resolve this asymmetry without making the comparison self-referential, the primary multimodal comparison used a within-study fMRI forward covariance pattern computed from observed held-out fMRI data and observed Level 2 residualized NPS scores:

$$A_{fMRI}^{NPS-L2} \propto \mathrm{Cov}(B_{fMRI}^{resid},\; y_{NPS-L2})$$

where $B_{fMRI}^{resid}$ denotes trial-wise fMRI beta maps after voxelwise residualization against the same Level 2 nuisance family used in Study 1: dummy-coded stimulus temperature, dummy-coded thermode contact-surface index (`selected_surface`), block/run index, trial onset, trial index, HRF-weighted `framewise_displacement`, HRF-weighted `std_dvars`, `peripheral_low_gamma_power`, and `residual_ecg_coupling`. The variable $y_{NPS-L2}$ denotes observed Level 2 residualized NPS expression. Voxelwise nuisance coefficients were estimated without the held-out subject in cross-validated comparisons to prevent leakage. The comparison using $\mathrm{Cov}(B_{fMRI},\; \hat{y}_{test})$ was retained only as a descriptive consistency check because it shares the same prediction vector as the EEG map and cannot establish pain specificity.

Specificity was tested as a superiority claim. The EEG source map had to align more strongly with $A_{fMRI}^{NPS-L2}$ than with non-pain control maps derived from circular-shifted NPS scores, HRF-weighted `framewise_displacement`, HRF-weighted `std_dvars`, `peripheral_low_gamma_power`, `residual_ecg_coupling`, stimulus-device timing variables, temperature, thermode contact-surface index (`selected_surface`), trial number, block, and pre-stimulus EEG predictions. No stimulation-laterality control map was specified because the stimulation site was fixed and the cohort did not contain a left/right stimulation manipulation; handedness was not modeled because all participants were right-handed. Subjective-rating maps were evaluated separately as criterion-overlap maps: strong alignment with ratings supported pain-report relevance but did not by itself establish fMRI pain-signature specificity. The primary specificity statistic was the paired spatial-correlation difference $\Delta r = |r(\text{EEG}, A_{fMRI}^{NPS-L2})| - |r(\text{EEG}, A_{control})|$ for each control map, tested under the same spin-permutation spatial null by rotating the EEG map and recomputing both correlations per permutation. A pattern was not designated pain-relevant if any control map had $\Delta r \leq 0$ in the observed data or if the Holm-corrected paired spatial-null test failed to show $\Delta r > 0$.

EEG source localization via sLORETA produces spatially smooth maps. Direct vertex-by-vertex comparison with a higher-resolution fMRI map would artificially depress the spatial correlation. The surface-mapped fMRI target was therefore smoothed using a Gaussian kernel matched to the sLORETA point-spread function, estimated before outcome-map inspection by applying the finalized forward/inverse operator to unit-amplitude cortical impulses distributed across the fsaverage mesh, measuring the geodesic full-width at half-maximum of each recovered source distribution, and taking the median across vertices within the cortical analysis mask. The primary smoothing kernel was fixed from this operator-only estimate (approximately FWHM $\approx 12$ mm) and was not tuned from observed EEG, fMRI, or behavioral validation maps.

Spatial similarity was evaluated using the Pearson correlation between continuous EEG source values and continuous fMRI target values across matched cortical vertices. Directionality was treated as an exploratory hypothesis: alpha and beta power may be negatively correlated with BOLD signal in some regions due to neurovascular coupling properties, but the sign depends on region, task, timing, baseline, and whether the analysis targets raw power, event-related desynchronization, or prediction covariance. No sign was hard-coded as indicating valid overlap.

Because adjacent cortical vertices are highly spatially autocorrelated, standard parametric p-values are invalid for brain-map comparisons. Statistical significance was determined using spherical spin permutations (Alexander-Bloch et al., 2018), implemented via a validated toolbox such as BrainSpace or neuromaps. The fsaverage spherical representation of the EEG map was randomly rotated 10,000 times relative to the fMRI target. The medial wall and non-cortical vertices were excluded from the surface mask. As a sensitivity analysis, alternative spatial null models (e.g., BrainSMASH variogram-matched surrogates) were evaluated.

## Internal Cross-Validated Behavioral Validation
To determine whether the derived EEG source-power covariance pattern captures pain-related variance beyond stimulus parameters within the same cohort, a LOSO behavioral validation was performed using a fixed scoring rule. This was internal cross-validated validation, not independent external validation. The primary validation pattern used the same alpha+beta full-plateau source-power covariance map as the confirmatory source inference, matching the gated Study 1 model cell. Alpha-only and beta-only full-plateau pattern-expression scores were tested as Holm-corrected secondary validation summaries to help interpret whether the combined pattern was driven primarily by one band. No frequency band, time window, vertex set, or cluster was selected from the Study 3 source-space significance map for the primary behavioral validation.

For each target subject, the group-level source-power covariance pattern at the primary band and time window was estimated from all remaining subjects. Trial-wise pattern expression was then computed for the held-out subject as:

$$\text{expression}_{s,i} = \sum_v \text{sourcepower}_{s,i}^{(\text{band})}(v) \cdot \text{Pattern}_{-s}^{(\text{band})}(v)$$

where $\text{sourcepower}_{s,i}^{(\text{band})}(v)$ denotes estimated source-power at vertex $v$ for subject $s$, trial $i$, and the primary alpha+beta frequency preset, time-averaged over the plateau window ($3.0$–$10.5$ s), and $\text{Pattern}_{-s}^{(\text{band})}(v)$ denotes the group-level source-power covariance pattern estimated without subject $s$ at the same band preset. This trial-wise expression score was then tested as a predictor of subjective pain ratings in the held-out subject after controlling for dummy-coded stimulus temperature, dummy-coded thermode contact-surface index (`selected_surface`), block/run index, trial onset, trial index, HRF-weighted `framewise_displacement`, HRF-weighted `std_dvars`, `peripheral_low_gamma_power`, and `residual_ecg_coupling`.

Pain versus non-pain classification was not a required validation endpoint because pain condition can be deterministic or near-deterministic with thermal manipulation. Classification was reported only as a secondary temperature-matched contrast when both classes were represented within the same retained temperature levels and the nuisance-adjusted design was full rank; otherwise it was declared unidentified. The internal rating validation was required before the pattern could be provisionally designated a candidate pain-relevant EEG source-power covariance pattern. The pattern was not designated an "EEG pain signature"; that label was reserved for a future independent cohort or untouched confirmatory split showing stimulus-intensity control, temporal negative-control specificity, artifact robustness, cross-subject generalization, and fixed-rule behavioral validity.

## Subject-Level Reporting
In addition to group-level source statistics, subject-wise Study 1 prediction performance, behavioral-validation coefficients, artifact metrics, retained trial counts, and map-expression distributions are reported with 95% bias-corrected and accelerated bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples) where the subject-level sample size supports stable estimation. Group-level summaries resampled subjects as the exchangeability unit. Within-subject intervals used run/block-level resampling when at least two runs were available and circular block bootstrap over trial order within run otherwise; trial-level iid bootstrap was not used because within-subject and within-run observations are autocorrelated.

## Artifact and Robustness Controls
All primary analyses were repeated under prespecified robustness conditions to determine whether the derived source-power covariance patterns reflect neural dynamics rather than structured artifacts. Trials were censored when any of the following thresholds were exceeded: framewise displacement $> 0.5$ mm, DVARS robust $z > 3$, temporal/frontal high-frequency electromyographic power robust $z > 3$, cardiac phase-locking above the 95th percentile of the within-subject circular-shift null, or scanner-frequency residual peaks robust $z > 3$. Analyses were repeated after equalizing trial counts across subjects and after excluding artifact-contaminated maps identified by sensor-level template checks. A candidate pain-relevant EEG pattern had to retain its sign and inferential status under these robustness analyses. Gamma-band maps remained exploratory even when they survived these controls.
