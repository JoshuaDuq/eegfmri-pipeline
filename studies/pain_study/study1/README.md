# Materials and Methods

## Research Objectives
This study tested whether trial-wise expression of established fMRI pain signatures can be predicted above chance from concurrent EEG-derived features. The predictive targets were the Neurologic Pain Signature (NPS; Wager et al., 2013) and the Stimulus Intensity Independent Pain Signature-1 (SIIPS1; Woo et al., 2017), modeled via a subject-level, trial-resolved multimodal architecture. The primary confirmatory estimand was prospective, leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) prediction of stimulus- and acquisition-controlled signature expression: NPS and SIIPS1 dot-product scores residualized against prespecified stimulus, thermode contact-surface, session, trial-order, motion, and artifact variables. Model fitting, target transformation, feature scaling, imputation, feature filtering, hyperparameter selection, and nuisance residualization used training subjects only. Subject-level EEG artifact correction and epoch QC used each participant's own raw EEG through fixed, unsupervised preprocessing rules and were not used to tune supervised prediction settings. Raw signature prediction was retained as a descriptive upper-bound analysis; subjective-rating residualization was treated as a construct-attenuation sensitivity analysis rather than a nuisance-controlled primary result.

The NPS was developed as a brain-based marker of nociceptive processing (Wager et al., 2013), and its prediction may partly reflect stimulus intensity and nociceptive input. The SIIPS1 captures stimulus-intensity-independent pain-related variance (Woo et al., 2017), making it conceptually closer to the residualized objective. SIIPS1 was nevertheless evaluated under the same residualization levels to verify independence in this dataset. All analyses were restricted to the plateau phase of thermal stimulation, including pain and non-pain thermal trials, utilizing fMRI inputs normalized to MNI152NLin2009cAsym space.

## Data Preconditions and Frequency Definitions
Analyses relied on task-evoked EEG epochs from $-7.0$ to $15.0$ s relative to stimulus onset. A $-0.2$ to $0.0$ s pre-stimulus baseline was applied to epoch-level voltage to remove DC offset prior to ERP and amplitude-based analyses. For time-frequency decompositions, the primary log-ratio baseline correction used the $-5.0$ to $-0.01$ s pre-stimulus window, selected to provide stable power estimates across low-frequency bands given that Morlet wavelet decomposition at alpha frequencies requires several cycles for adequate spectral resolution. Because this window may contain cue-locked expectancy activity, baseline sensitivity analyses were prespecified using two alternative windows: an immediate pre-stimulus baseline ($-0.2$ to $-0.01$ s) and an early pre-stimulus baseline ($-7.0$ to $-5.5$ s). The early-window analysis was interpreted only when event timing confirmed it preceded task cues. Baseline conclusions were considered stable only if the sign and inferential status of primary alpha and beta effects were unchanged across the primary and immediate-baseline analyses. All baseline windows were applied identically across pain and non-pain conditions.

The active stimulation window for predictive modeling was restricted to $3.0$–$10.5$ s post-onset. Neural oscillations were operationalized into standard frequency bands: alpha ($8.0$–$12.9$ Hz), beta ($13.0$–$30.0$ Hz), and gamma ($30.1$–$80.0$ Hz). Composite features spanning the full alpha-to-gamma spectrum were also constructed by concatenating these ranges. Gamma-band EEG recorded during simultaneous fMRI is vulnerable to facial muscle activity, jaw tension, scanner gradient residuals, and cardiac artifacts, all of which can be elicited by painful stimulation. Gamma-band analyses were therefore exploratory by design and were not used to support confirmatory pain-signature claims. Gamma robustness was evaluated using temporal/frontal high-frequency electromyographic power, framewise displacement, DVARS, cardiac phase-locking, and residual spectral peaks at the volume-repetition frequency or its harmonics. Prespecified artifact thresholds were: framewise displacement $> 0.5$ mm, temporal/frontal high-frequency power robust $z > 3$, DVARS robust $z > 3$, cardiac phase-locking above the 95th percentile of the within-subject circular-shift null, and scanner-frequency residual peaks robust $z > 3$. Gamma effects were reported as artifact-sensitive when removing threshold-exceeding trials changed the effect direction or eliminated significance. The gamma band structurally excludes a $\pm 1.0$ Hz notch around 60 Hz due to line-noise removal; harmonics at 120 Hz and above fall outside the 100 Hz low-pass cutoff.

## Simultaneous EEG-fMRI Preprocessing
Simultaneous EEG-fMRI acquisition introduces structured artifacts requiring correction prior to feature extraction (Bullock et al., 2021). Preprocessing proceeded in two stages.

In the first stage, MRI-induced artifacts were corrected using BrainVision Analyzer 2.0 (Brain Products GmbH, Gilching, Germany). Gradient artifacts from rapid magnetic-field-gradient switching during echo-planar imaging were removed via average artifact subtraction, in which a sliding-window template of the gradient waveform was estimated and subtracted from each volume acquisition window. Ballistocardiogram (BCG) artifacts—caused by pulse-related electrode motion within the static magnetic field—were corrected via template subtraction aligned to the detected cardiac cycle.

In the second stage, the gradient- and BCG-corrected data were imported into the MNE-BIDS-Pipeline for standard EEG preprocessing. Continuous data were downsampled to 500 Hz. A 0.1 Hz high-pass filter, a 100 Hz low-pass filter, and a 60 Hz notch filter were applied.

Bad channels were identified using PyPREP (Bigdely-Shamlo et al., 2015), which applies automated deviation-based and correlation-based rejection criteria with optional RANSAC validation. Detection was repeated three times with independent random seeds, and a channel was marked bad only if flagged in a strict majority of runs. Detected bad channels were synchronized across runs per subject using a per-run union policy, then interpolated via spherical spline interpolation. Data were subsequently re-referenced to the common average.

Independent component analysis (ICA) was performed using the extended infomax algorithm with 0.99 variance explained as the retention criterion. ICA was fitted on epochs high-pass filtered at 1.0 Hz to improve decomposition stability. Component classification was performed using ICLabel (Pion-Tonachini et al., 2019); components were rejected if their predicted probability exceeded 0.8 for any non-brain category other than "other" (i.e., only components classified as "brain" or "other" were retained). Because ICLabel was trained on standard (non-fMRI) EEG data, residual gradient artifact fragments, BCG template-subtraction residuals, and scanner-related cable-motion artifacts may be classified as "other" and retained. As a supplementary quality-control step, retained components were inspected for spectral peaks at the volume-repetition frequency or its harmonics, cardiac phase-locking exceeding chance, and spatial topographies consistent with known artifact patterns. The number of rejected components and their ICLabel classifications were logged per subject.

Epochs were extracted from $-7.0$ to $15.0$ s relative to stimulus onset. Trial rejection was performed using autoreject (Jas et al., 2017) in local mode, which determines channel-specific peak-to-peak amplitude thresholds via cross-validation and repairs or rejects individual channels within individual epochs. The number of candidate interpolations per trial was set to $\{4, 8, 16\}$. Rejected and interpolated trial counts per subject were logged. Electrode locations were digitized using the EasyCap M1 montage and co-registered to individual MRI.

## fMRI Signature Target Construction
Trial-by-trial fMRI effect estimates were extracted using the Least-Squares Separate (LSS) generalized linear modeling approach, restricted to stimulation-phase trials during the thermal plateau. The LSS event regressor onset was defined as the beginning of the thermal plateau (after ramp-up), with event duration equal to the plateau hold time. Ramp-up and ramp-down periods were modeled as separate nuisance regressors to prevent their variance from contaminating plateau-specific estimates; all remaining non-stimulation trial types were similarly modeled as nuisance regressors. This design aligns the modeled fMRI event with the same protocol-defined plateau phase used for EEG feature extraction, but the resulting LSS beta is an HRF-convolved event-amplitude estimate and should not be interpreted as temporally equivalent to millisecond-scale EEG activity.

The primary LSS models used a canonical SPM hemodynamic response function, a cosine drift model, and a $0.008$ Hz high-pass filter without additional spatial smoothing. fMRIPrep confounds were required for every included run. The prespecified fMRI denoising design required the 24-parameter rigid-body motion expansion, white-matter and CSF signals, `framewise_displacement`, the first five fMRIPrep CompCor regressors selected by the frozen confound-column policy, and all generated fMRIPrep non-steady-state or motion-outlier regressors. Missing, nonnumeric, or nonfinite selected confounds failed target construction. Trial-level fMRI QC recorded HRF-weighted `framewise_displacement` and `std_dvars` for every plateau trial; trials exceeding framewise displacement $> 0.5$ mm or standardized DVARS robust $z > 3$ were excluded from confirmatory prediction, and any run losing more than 20% of plateau trials to fMRI QC failed run-level inclusion. HRF/timing robustness was evaluated by repeating target construction with canonical-HRF temporal and dispersion derivatives and by testing a finite-impulse-response model centered on the plateau; EEG prediction was also repeated with shifted active windows displaced by $\pm 2.0$ s from the primary $3.0$–$10.5$ s window. A result was considered HRF/timing-stable only if the Level 2 ElasticNet estimate retained the same sign for both co-primary targets, the absolute performance estimate changed by no more than 25%, and the Holm-corrected inferential status was unchanged. Results failing any criterion were reported as HRF/timing-sensitive and could not support the primary physiological interpretation.

The NPS weight map was the group-level cross-validated principal component regression weights (`weights_NSF_grouppred_cvpcr.nii.gz`; Wager et al., 2013); the SIIPS1 map was the 137-subject weighted mean (`nonnoc_v11_4_137subjmap_weighted_mean.nii.gz`; Woo et al., 2017). Both were registered to MNI152NLin2009cAsym space.

When image grids differed, LSS beta maps were resampled to the signature-weight grid using continuous interpolation, and brain masks were resampled with nearest-neighbor interpolation. For each signature, the scoring mask $V^{(k)}$ was fixed by the finite signature-weight voxels intersected with the resampled fMRIPrep brain mask. The contributing voxel count was required to be identical across all retained subjects, runs, and trials for that signature. A trial was retained only when every voxel in the fixed mask had a finite LSS beta value; trials with missing or non-finite values inside $V^{(k)}$ failed target quality control rather than being scored on a smaller trial-specific voxel set. The fixed contributing voxel count was logged for every target; any subject whose QC exclusions left insufficient retained trials failed analysis-level inclusion.

LSS beta maps were not spatially smoothed, z-scored, or trial-normalized before signature scoring; signature weights were not re-estimated from the study data. Let $\beta_{s,i}(v)$ denote the LSS-derived BOLD effect estimate for subject $s$, trial $i$, and voxel $v$, and let $M_k(v)$ denote the a priori weight map for target $k \in \{\text{NPS}, \text{SIIPS1}\}$. The primary signature-expression metric was computed as the unscaled dot product across the fixed masked voxel set:

$$y_{s,i}^{(k)} = \sum_{v \in V^{(k)}} \beta_{s,i}(v) \, M_k(v)$$

To construct the multimodal dataset, fMRI targets $y_{s,i}^{(k)}$ were matched to clean EEG trials using unique subject, run/block, and trial-index identifiers. Exact trial identifiers were required for confirmatory alignment; missing, duplicate, or one-to-many EEG-fMRI matches failed fast. Rounded onset-duration agreement was retained only as an audit check after trial-identifier matching. EEG trigger onsets and fMRI plateau onsets were compared after applying the prespecified protocol offset from trigger to plateau start; the residual absolute mismatch was required to be $\leq 0.010$ s for every retained trial. Trials exceeding this threshold were excluded; any run with more than 1% excluded temporal-audit failures or maximum residual mismatch $>0.050$ s failed run-level alignment QC. Temporal mismatch audits did not create a fallback matching route or justify averaging duplicate trials.

## Hierarchical Nuisance Residualization
To characterize whether EEG prediction reflects pain-relevant fMRI variance or confound-correlated variance, fMRI signature expression was evaluated at three prespecified residualization levels. These levels are distinct estimands rather than interchangeable preprocessing options. A level was analyzed only when its nuisance design was full rank within every training fold; rank-deficient designs failed fast rather than being silently simplified.

**Level 1 — Raw expression.** No residualization. Models predict the full NPS/SIIPS1 dot-product expression, which reflects stimulus intensity, condition, temporal structure, subjective experience, and neural pain processing in an unknown mixture.

**Level 2 — Stimulus- and acquisition-controlled expression.** Targets were residualized against a design matrix containing an intercept, dummy-coded stimulus temperature levels (`stimulus_temp`, with the lowest observed retained temperature as reference), dummy-coded thermode contact-surface index (`selected_surface`, levels 1-5 from the PsychoPy behavior file), block/run index (`block`), trial onset time (`onset`), within-run trial number (`trial_index`), HRF-weighted `framewise_displacement`, HRF-weighted `std_dvars`, `peripheral_low_gamma_power`, and `residual_ecg_coupling`. The confirmatory Level 2 target table required these columns to be present and finite; missing fields failed analysis rather than being silently dropped. Temperature and `selected_surface` were modeled categorically because calibrated thermal levels and thermode contact surfaces are discrete. `selected_surface` was treated as a contact-surface nuisance variable, not an anatomical stimulation-site or stimulation-laterality variable: the stimulation site was fixed across trials. Handedness was also not modeled because all participants were right-handed and handedness is not a trial-level nuisance in this cohort. Binary pain condition was excluded from the primary nuisance design because it is a deterministic or near-deterministic recoding of the thermal manipulation in this paradigm and can render the estimand unidentified. Rating-scale bounds (`vas_scale_min`, `vas_scale_max`) were not included in the primary nuisance design because they are determined by the pain/non-pain rating branch; they were evaluated only in a separately reported full-rank sensitivity design. Trial duration was not included because target construction already restricts trials to the plateau phase and models plateau duration in the LSS event definition. Level 2 is the primary confirmatory target: it asks whether EEG predicts signature variance beyond measured stimulus level, thermode contact surface, session structure, trial order, and measured motion/artifact burden.

**Level 3 — Rating-residualized sensitivity.** The Level 2 design was augmented with subjective pain rating (`vas_final_coded_rating`). Because subjective ratings are a criterion measure of pain experience rather than a pure nuisance variable, this level may remove construct-relevant variance. Level 3 was interpreted only as a sensitivity analysis testing whether EEG predicts residual fMRI signature variance after removing both stimulus/session effects and the subject's own pain report; it was not treated as definitive nuisance-control evidence.

Within each level, nuisance coefficients $\hat{\gamma}$ were estimated exclusively on training subjects via SVD-based least squares to prevent test-subject leakage:

$$\hat{\gamma} = \underset{\gamma}{\mathrm{argmin}} \; \| y_{train} - Z_{train}\gamma \|_2^2$$

Nuisance predictors were checked for multicollinearity before fitting. The block indicator was encoded as a numeric order term, imposing a linear drift assumption. Dummy-coded block indicators, polynomial trial-order terms, and rating-scale-bound sensitivities were evaluated only as separately reported sensitivity estimands where the design was full rank; they did not replace the primary Level 2 design. Residualized targets for both training and test sets were computed by applying the training-derived coefficients:

$$y_{train}^{resid} = y_{train} - Z_{train}\hat{\gamma}, \qquad y_{test}^{resid} = y_{test} - Z_{test}\hat{\gamma}$$

If prediction was significant at Level 1 but not Level 2, EEG features were interpreted as primarily tracking stimulus or session structure rather than pain-related fMRI signature variance. If prediction survived Level 2 but not Level 3, the prediction was interpreted as overlapping with subjective pain-report variance, not as a failure of the primary pain-relevant hypothesis.

## EEG Feature Extraction
Spectral power features were extracted from band-passed EEG epochs using Morlet wavelet decomposition with frequency-adaptive cycle counts (cycles $= f / 2.0$, bounded between 3.0 and 15.0) and decimation factor 4. To isolate induced (non-phase-locked) oscillatory activity, the evoked response (ERP) was subtracted from each trial prior to time-frequency decomposition. In the primary analysis, the ERP was estimated as the fold-level average across all training trials regardless of condition. As a sensitivity analysis, condition-specific ERP subtraction was also evaluated, acknowledging that condition-specific subtraction removes condition-mean variance and may attenuate condition-related prediction if pain condition is related to the target. In both cases, the ERP was computed exclusively from training-fold trials to prevent cross-validation leakage.

Spectral power was log-ratio baseline-corrected against the pre-stimulus window ($-5.0$ to $-0.01$ s) and averaged within the active stimulation window ($3.0$ to $10.5$ s) to yield one power value per trial, channel, and frequency band. Line noise at 60 Hz was excluded from gamma-band averaging using a $\pm 1.0$ Hz notch; harmonics above the 100 Hz low-pass cutoff were not present in the data. Power features were computed at three spatial resolutions: individual channels, predefined regions of interest (frontal, sensorimotor left/right, temporal left/right, parieto-occipital left/right/midline), and global average.

An exploratory feature set additionally encompassed spectral peak frequency and bandwidth, aperiodic slope and offset (FOOOF/specparam; fixed model), event-related desynchronization and synchronization, band power ratios ($\theta/\beta$, $\theta/\alpha$, $\alpha/\beta$, $\delta/\alpha$, $\delta/\theta$), hemispheric alpha asymmetry, nonlinear complexity measures (permutation entropy, sample entropy, Lempel-Ziv complexity), and oscillatory burst statistics.

## Feature-Based Machine Learning Architecture
A rigorously nested LOSO cross-validation framework was employed to ensure true out-of-sample generalization. The primary confirmatory model was ElasticNet regression on spectral power features, selected for its sparsity-inducing regularization and interpretability in high-dimensional settings. Ridge regression was included as a secondary confirmatory model. Random Forest was designated exploratory due to its sensitivity to correlated predictors in high-dimensional, low-subject neuroimaging settings and weaker biological interpretability relative to linear models.

To prevent cross-subject data leakage, preprocessing steps were applied independently within each outer training fold. Let $X$ represent the trial-by-feature matrix. The primary preprocessing lane was non-transductive: feature medians, means, standard deviations, and variance-threshold decisions were estimated from outer training subjects only, then applied unchanged to the held-out subject. Missing trials were imputed using training-cohort medians. Features were standardized to zero mean and unit variance using training-fold statistics, and a variance threshold of $0.0$ removed features constant in the training fold. Session-normalized scaling using all held-out subject trials was excluded from the primary benchmark; if run as a separate calibration analysis, it must be labeled transductive and cannot support the primary claim.

Processed fMRI targets were normalized using the Yeo-Johnson power transformation, denoted $\tilde{y} = \mathrm{YJ}(y)$. Transformation parameters were fit on outer training targets only and applied to the held-out subject. Predictions were inverse-transformed before reporting primary $R^2$, root-mean-squared error, and calibration summaries on the active target scale. Transformed-scale metrics were reported as optimization diagnostics. The ElasticNet objective was:

$$\min_{\beta_0,\beta} \frac{1}{2n} \left\| \tilde{y} - \beta_0 - X\beta \right\|_2^2 + \alpha \rho \|\beta\|_1 + \frac{\alpha(1-\rho)}{2}\|\beta\|_2^2$$

The Ridge objective was:

$$\min_{\beta_0,\beta} \frac{1}{2n} \left\| \tilde{y} - \beta_0 - X\beta \right\|_2^2 + \frac{\alpha}{2}\|\beta\|_2^2$$

Hyperparameters were tuned via a 5-fold inner group cross-validation loop restricted to training subjects, with validation folds defined at the subject level via GroupKFold. The software minimum for this scheme is 6 analyzable subjects (every LOSO training fold must contain at least 5 subjects for inner GroupKFold). Confirmatory inference additionally required at least 20 analyzable subjects or a preregistered precision simulation showing a 95% confidence-interval half-width $\leq 0.10$ for the primary out-of-sample $R^2$ under the observed trial structure. Cohorts meeting only the computational minimum were reported as descriptive feasibility analyses.

For ElasticNet, regularization strength $\alpha$ was tuned over $\{0.001, 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10\}$ and L1 ratio $\rho$ over $\{0.2, 0.5, 0.8\}$, with a maximum of 10,000 iterations and cyclic coordinate descent. For Ridge, $\alpha$ was tuned over $\{0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0\}$. Random Forest used 500 bootstrap estimators, with maximum tree depths of $\{5, 10, 20, \text{None}\}$, minimum samples to split of $\{2, 5, 10\}$, and minimum samples per leaf of $\{1, 2, 4\}$.

## Deep Regression Neural Architecture
An exploratory deep regression lane was implemented to learn directly from continuous, band-limited EEG dynamics. Because the independent unit for LOSO generalization is the subject and the cohort size may be modest, the deep network is designated exploratory unless the subject count is sufficient to justify confirmatory status.

Continuous EEG recordings were band-pass filtered into target frequency ranges, and instantaneous power was extracted via the analytic signal envelope using the Hilbert transform. The resulting tensors were cropped to the active stimulation window ($3.0$–$10.5$ s), yielding a four-dimensional input tensor of shape $N \times B \times C \times T$ (trials $\times$ bands $\times$ channels $\times$ time). Within each LOSO fold, input tensors were standardized using channel- and band-specific training-cohort statistics:

$$\tilde{X}_{n,b,c,t} = \frac{X_{n,b,c,t} - \mu_{b,c}}{\sigma_{b,c}^{*}}$$

where $\mu_{b,c}$ is the training mean and $\sigma_{b,c}^{*}$ is the standard deviation lower-bounded at $10^{-6}$. Target variables were z-scored using training-subset statistics.

A custom PyTorch convolutional neural network was designed to enforce neurophysiological constraints by isolating spatial topographies prior to temporal integration. The depthwise spatial convolution utilized a kernel spanning all EEG channels with independent filter groups per frequency band, producing output of shape $N \times B \times F \times T$:

$$H^{(1)}_{n,b,f,t} = \mathrm{ELU}\left(\mathrm{BN}\left(\sum_{c} W^{spat}_{b,f,c}\tilde{X}_{n,b,c,t}\right)\right)$$

where $f$ indexes spatial filters. A temporal convolution with kernel length 15 and 8 temporal filters then integrated across bands and time, producing output of shape $N \times F' \times T$:

$$H^{(2)}_{n,f',t} = \mathrm{ELU}\left(\mathrm{BN}\left(\sum_{b,f,u} W^{temp}_{f',b,f,u} H^{(1)}_{n,b,f,t+u}\right)\right)$$

where $f'$ indexes temporal filters. The latent temporal representation was downsampled via average pooling (factor 8) and passed through a dropout-regularized ($p = 0.25$) fully connected regression head:

$$z_n = \mathrm{Pool}_{8}\left(\mathrm{Dropout}(H^{(2)}_n)\right)$$

$$\hat{y}_n = W_2 \, \mathrm{Dropout}\left(\mathrm{ELU}\left(W_1 \, \mathrm{vec}(z_n) + b_1\right)\right) + b_2$$

The network was optimized using AdamW (learning rate $= 0.001$, weight decay $= 0.0001$) minimizing mean squared error over 25 epochs with batch size 32. Twenty percent of training subjects were reserved as a held-out validation set for patience-based early stopping (patience $= 5$ epochs); no trials from validation subjects were used during network optimization. Deep-regression folds required at least 10 training subjects so that the validation split contained at least two subjects; folds below this threshold failed deep-regression QC.

## Evaluation Metrics and Statistical Inference
Model performance was evaluated on the held-out test subject during each LOSO iteration. Primary metrics were computed on the active target scale after inverse-transforming predictions where a target transform was used. The coefficient of determination was calculated using the training-fold target mean ($\bar{y}_{train,f}$) as the zero-skill baseline:

$$R_f^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i \in f}(y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_{i \in f}(y_i - \bar{y}_{train,f})^2}$$

The primary confirmatory performance statistic was the subject-weighted mean LOSO $R^2$ on the inverse-transformed active target scale. Each held-out subject contributed one $R_f^2$ value per target, model, and frequency preset; the group statistic was the arithmetic mean across held-out subjects, giving each subject equal weight regardless of retained trial count. A confirmatory cell was considered positive only when this mean $R^2$ was greater than zero and its permutation $p$ value survived Holm correction within the prespecified confirmatory family. Pearson correlation, Spearman correlation, root-mean-squared error, calibration slope, and subject-wise $R^2$ distributions were reported as secondary descriptive metrics. Subject-wise $R^2$, Pearson correlation, Spearman correlation, and root-mean-squared error are reported with 95% bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples) to characterize individual variability. Group-level confidence intervals resampled subjects as the exchangeability unit. Within-subject intervals used run/block-level resampling when at least two runs were available and circular block bootstrap over trial order within run otherwise; trial-level iid bootstrap was not used. Given the small effective sample size at the subject level in LOSO designs, bootstrap confidence intervals may be unstable, and this limitation is acknowledged.

Statistical inference was established via nonparametric permutation testing with 5,000 permutations. The permutation null operated on nuisance-residualized targets, not on raw signature scores. Within each outer fold and residualization level, nuisance coefficients were estimated from training subjects, applied to both training and held-out subjects, and the resulting residual targets were circularly shifted relative to EEG features within each subject and run with a minimum shift distance of 5 trials. This residual-permutation strategy preserves the observed nuisance design, avoids breaking the target-nuisance relationship before residualization, and tests whether EEG predicts the nuisance-controlled target better than expected under within-run temporal exchangeability. Circular shifts approximately preserve within-block temporal structure including habituation, carryover, and local drift (Winkler et al., 2014). The number of valid unique circular shifts per subject per run was logged; runs with fewer than 10 valid trials were excluded from permutation inference. For each permutation, the full outer-fold prediction pipeline after residualization was refit, including imputation, scaling, target transformation, feature selection, hyperparameter tuning, and model fitting. As a sensitivity analysis, within-block random shuffling of residual targets—which preserves block membership but destroys within-block autocorrelation—was also evaluated.

The Holm-corrected confirmatory family was defined before model fitting as 2 fMRI targets (NPS, SIIPS1) $\times$ 2 confirmatory linear models (ElasticNet, Ridge) $\times$ the primary Level 2 residualization target $\times$ 3 prespecified spectral-power frequency presets: alpha, beta, and alpha+beta. Gamma and alpha+beta+gamma presets were excluded from the confirmatory family and reported only as exploratory artifact-sensitive analyses. Level 1, Level 3, Random Forest, deep-regression, alternative nuisance designs, and temporal/baseline sensitivity analyses were reported outside this primary family.

Computational reproducibility was supported by logging all random seeds (global seed $= 42$), software versions, data hashes, and deterministic computation settings. Exact trial yields, participant inclusions, and selected hyperparameters were programmatically logged.

## Nuisance Prediction Reporting
To assess how much EEG prediction could reflect measured confounds or pain-report overlap, supplementary models were trained to predict stimulus temperature, thermode contact-surface index (`selected_surface`), trial number, block/run index, `framewise_displacement`, `std_dvars`, `peripheral_low_gamma_power`, `residual_ecg_coupling`, and subjective pain ratings from the same EEG features. Strong prediction of stimulus, contact-surface, session, motion, or artifact variables indicated caution that the model may be tracking task structure or acquisition noise. Strong prediction of subjective ratings was interpreted as construct overlap with pain report rather than as a pure nuisance result. The Level 2 residualization framework provides the primary evidence: EEG features must predict stimulus- and acquisition-controlled fMRI pain-signature expression above chance before any pain-relevant interpretation is made.

## Temporal Negative Controls
To assess temporal specificity, models were trained using EEG features from the pre-stimulus window ($-5.0$ to $0.0$ s) and the immediate pre-stimulus window ($-0.2$ to $0.0$ s) to predict post-stimulus NPS expression. If either pre-stimulus model predicted the pain signature comparably to stimulation-period EEG, the model was interpreted as capturing expectancy, block structure, or subject-level state rather than nociceptive processing. Wrong-lag windows were prespecified as ramp-up ($0.0$–$3.0$ s), late ramp-down/post-stimulation ($10.5$–$15.0$ s), early-shifted active ($1.0$–$8.5$ s), and late-shifted active ($5.0$–$12.5$ s). These windows were evaluated without reselecting frequency bands, channels, or model hyperparameters after inspecting outcomes.
