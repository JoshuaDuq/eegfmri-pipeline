# Study 1 Sensor-Topography Figures Design

## Summary

Add two standalone Study 1 publication figures that show spatially resolved scalp-power
associations across the five prespecified bands:

1. `sensor_power_topographies.svg`: delivered-temperature and subjective-intensity effects.
2. `signature_power_topographies.svg`: NPS and SIIPS1 associations.

Both figures use participants as the independent inferential units, retain unthresholded effect
maps, and mark only sensor clusters that survive family-wise correction across all ten maps in the
corresponding figure. The figures are descriptive sensor-space analyses and do not claim cortical
source localization.

## Goals

- Show scientifically interpretable topographies for alpha, beta, low gamma, mid gamma, and high
  gamma.
- Align every estimand, band, trial, nuisance variable, and channel scope with the existing Study 1
  protocol.
- Control spatial, band, and row multiplicity within each figure as one inferential family.
- Preserve every plotted and inferred quantity in machine-readable audit artifacts.
- Run independently after the required Study 1 target and feature artifacts exist.

## Non-goals

- Do not change Study 1 preprocessing, target preparation, feature extraction, prediction, or
  reporting behavior.
- Do not infer cortical generators from sensor topographies.
- Do not interpret univariate sensor-target associations as multivariate model importance.
- Do not add delta, theta, combined-band, time-resolved, source-space, or Study 2 maps.
- Do not add article-readiness gates, preliminary labels, or sample-size-based interpretation.
- Do not add uncorrected significance markers or inferential fallbacks.

## Prerequisites and execution

The figure commands require existing:

- preprocessed EEG and clean event tables;
- `signature-prediction prepare-targets` outputs;
- `signature-prediction prepare-features` trial-safe power tables.

They do not require `feature-benchmark`, `deep-regression`, or `report`. Each figure is generated by
its own Python module and never launches upstream work. Missing or inconsistent artifacts surface as
errors naming the failed input.

Default outputs live under the configured Study 1 supplementary-validity directory. The commands
accept the same main configuration, Study 1 configuration, derivative root, task, and optional SVG
output path conventions as the existing standalone validity figures.

## Shared input representation

The implementation reads the exact retained target/event population through the current Study 1
validity-data loader and reads each retained participant's `features_power.parquet` table.

For every trial, band, and channel, active-versus-baseline power is reconstructed in dB from the
validated channel-level baseline and log-ratio columns. The loader requires:

- the five bands to equal `PRIMARY_BAND_PRESETS["alpha_beta_gamma"]` in order;
- identical baseline and active channel sets within and across bands;
- unique integer trial identifiers;
- finite, positive linear baseline and reconstructed active power;
- identical retained trial alignment between events, targets, and power features;
- valid, unique montage coordinates for every analyzed EEG channel.

ECG and other non-EEG channels never enter a topomap. The shared loader returns a tidy trial table
with subject, trial, band, channel, channel power in dB, behavioral variables, signature targets,
and the configured nuisance columns.

## Figure 1: sensor power construct topographies

### Layout

`sensor_power_topographies.svg` is a fixed 2-by-5 figure:

- row 1: delivered-temperature effect;
- row 2: subjective-intensity effect;
- columns: alpha, beta, low gamma, mid gamma, and high gamma.

### Temperature estimand

Within each participant, band, and channel:

1. Average channel power within each of the six configured temperature conditions.
2. Require every configured temperature cell.
3. Fit the linear slope across condition means.

The participant estimand is dB per 1 degree Celsius. Equal weighting of temperature-condition
means prevents unequal retained trial counts from changing the temperature-level weighting.

### Subjective-intensity estimand

Within each participant, band, and channel, compute the partial correlation between channel power
and the continuous within-scale 0-100 intensity score. Residualize both variables against the exact
power construct-validity nuisance design:

- categorical temperature;
- run;
- thermode surface;
- within-run trial order;
- residual ECG coupling;
- the Fp1/Fp2 high-frequency artifact proxy.

The model uses the current minimum-trial, minimum-run, rank, residual-degrees-of-freedom, and
condition-number validation rules. Participant correlations are Fisher-z transformed for group
inference and back-transformed for display.

### Channel scope and sensitivity

The primary figure uses the channel-inclusion setting configured for
`study1.figures.power_construct_validity`, including Fp1/Fp2 under the current configuration. When
the existing complementary channel sensitivity is enabled, its scope is the Boolean complement of
the configured primary Fp1/Fp2 setting. Under the current Fp1/Fp2-included primary configuration,
this is the Fp1/Fp2-excluded analysis. Participant effects and cohort summaries are written as
descriptive sensitivity audits with the actual inclusion state recorded. They do not form an
additional visual or inferential family.

## Figure 2: fMRI-signature power topographies

### Layout

`signature_power_topographies.svg` is a fixed 2-by-5 figure:

- row 1: NPS association;
- row 2: SIIPS1 association;
- columns: alpha, beta, low gamma, mid gamma, and high gamma.

### Signature estimands

Within each participant, band, and channel, compute a partial correlation between channel power and
the raw trial-wise signature expression. Residualize both power and the signature against the exact
target-specific Level 2 nuisance columns returned by
`resolve_target_residualization_columns`:

- NPS uses the configured acquisition, timing, physiological, artifact, temperature, and surface
  nuisance design;
- SIIPS1 uses that same design plus NPS, matching the prespecified SIIPS1 nuisance branch.

Participant correlations are Fisher-z transformed for group inference and back-transformed for
display. The caption identifies these as univariate sensor-target associations, not predictive
model coefficients, feature importance, or source localization.

### Participant-level signature design contract

For each target, the participant design starts with an intercept and the ordered columns returned
by `resolve_target_residualization_columns`. The implementation then applies this fixed
within-participant contract:

- require at least
  `study1.feature_benchmark.circular_shift.min_retained_trials_per_subject` retained rows and
  `study1.feature_benchmark.circular_shift.min_valid_runs_per_subject` runs;
- require finite target, power, and nuisance values;
- identify nuisance columns with zero within-participant variance, omit them because they are
  exactly absorbed by the participant intercept, and record their names in the participant audit;
- center and scale each remaining nuisance column to unit Euclidean norm;
- require more rows than design parameters and positive residual degrees of freedom;
- require the centered, scaled nuisance design to have every singular-value ratio at least
  `study1.figures.sensor_topographies.signature_model.rank_tolerance`;
- require the full standardized design condition number not to exceed
  `study1.figures.sensor_topographies.signature_model.max_condition_number`;
- residualize power and the target against the same validated design;
- require both residual vectors to have standard deviation greater than
  `study1.figures.sensor_topographies.signature_model.minimum_residual_standard_deviation`.

The Study 1 figure configuration sets `rank_tolerance: 1.0e-10`,
`max_condition_number: 100.0`, and `minimum_residual_standard_deviation: 1.0e-12` explicitly. There
is no pseudoinverse, column-selection heuristic, or alternate model after a validation failure.

### Channel scope

The signature figure excludes `study1.feature_benchmark.excluded_channels`, currently Fp1 and Fp2,
because the maps describe the individual-channel predictors eligible for the confirmatory
signature-prediction analysis. The command rejects an empty or montage-incompatible resolved
channel set.

## Complete-case family population

Each figure resolves its population independently. A participant enters a figure's primary
inference only when every required participant effect is finite for all two rows, five bands, and
analyzed sensors. This produces one fixed participant population and one fixed sensor adjacency for
the entire ten-map family.

Participant-level non-estimability is an audited exclusion, not a command-fatal structural error.
It includes too few retained trials or runs, a missing temperature cell, nonpositive residual
degrees of freedom, participant-design rank or condition failure, zero residual variance, or any
other failure to produce the complete ten-map participant effect grid. Failure in any cell excludes
that participant from the full figure family and records the exact reason and affected cell.

Malformed or inconsistent source artifacts remain command-fatal. Examples include missing files or
columns, duplicate or misaligned trials, unexpected bands or global channel disagreement, invalid
feature names, non-finite source power, and incompatible montage metadata. These indicate invalid
inputs rather than participant-level scientific non-estimability and must be corrected upstream.

The pipeline does not remove individual electrodes or use map-specific participant samples. It
reports the included participant count without an article-readiness judgment.

Inference requires enough participants for a one-sample statistic and enough distinct two-sided
sign patterns to resolve the configured family alpha. Failure of either condition surfaces as an
error rather than producing uncorrected results.

## Cluster inference

Each figure is one inferential family containing ten maps. The observed statistic is a one-sample
t-statistic across participants at every sensor. The algorithm:

1. Loads the standard montage named by `preprocessing.montage`, requires one finite unique 3D
   position for every analyzed channel, takes the montage's top-view x-y coordinates, and computes
   a SciPy Delaunay triangulation. Two sensors are adjacent exactly when they share a triangle edge;
   the resulting undirected binary matrix includes a true diagonal and must form one connected
   graph. Qhull failure, duplicate projected positions, or graph disconnection is fatal. The
   implementation does not call the existing adjacency helper because that helper has a distance
   fallback.
2. Converts the two-sided cluster-forming probability of 0.01 to positive and negative t
   thresholds using the observed degrees of freedom.
3. Forms connected positive and negative sensor clusters separately.
4. Defines cluster mass as the sum of absolute sensor t-statistics in a cluster.
5. Applies one synchronized sign to a participant's entire 10-by-sensor effect tensor on each null
   draw, preserving dependence across rows, bands, and sensors.
6. Records the largest cluster mass across all ten maps for each draw.
7. Compares every observed cluster with that joint maximum distribution.

Inference settings live under `study1.figures.sensor_topographies.inference` and explicitly define
`cluster_forming_p: 0.01`, `family_alpha: 0.05`, `max_null_draws: 10000`, and
`seed: 20260715`. The complete two-sided sign space fixes the first participant's sign positive, so
it contains `2 ** (n_participants - 1)` canonical patterns.

When the canonical pattern count is at most `max_null_draws`, the implementation enumerates the
complete two-sided sign-flip space exactly and reports the actual count. Exact enumeration includes
the observed all-positive labeling; its p-value is the number of canonical maxima at least as large
as the observed cluster mass divided by the total canonical pattern count. When the complete space
is larger, the implementation samples 10,000 unique canonical patterns excluding the observed
labeling and uses `(1 + exceedances) / 10001`.
The audit distinguishes requested maximum draws, sampled null draws, total exact patterns, and
whether the observed labeling is included. This is an explicit permutation rule, not an
inferential fallback.

Clusters survive when their corrected two-sided family-wise p-value is at most 0.05. A map with no
surviving cluster remains visible without an overlay. No channelwise, FDR, asymptotic, or
uncorrected result substitutes for the configured family test.

## Visual design

Both figures use the established Study 1 publication style, fixed physical dimensions, Arial text,
and a zero-centered diverging color map.

- Temperature maps share one symmetric dB-per-degree-Celsius scale across their row.
- Subjective-intensity maps share one symmetric partial-r scale across their row.
- NPS and SIIPS1 maps share one symmetric partial-r scale across the complete signature figure.
- Display limits equal the maximum absolute displayed cohort effect within the corresponding shared
  scale. No percentile clipping is allowed.
- Topographic interpolation is descriptive; inference remains defined at measured sensors.
- Sensors in a surviving corrected cluster receive dark rings. The renderer does not interpolate a
  significance field or draw a scalp region that implies unmeasured spatial precision.
- Cluster identifiers, signs, extents, masses, and corrected p-values live in the caption and audit
  tables instead of crowding map titles.

## Components

Implementation is divided by responsibility:

1. A shared channel-power data module validates and reconstructs tidy trial-level sensor power.
2. A construct-estimand module builds temperature and subjective-intensity participant maps and
   descriptive channel sensitivity.
3. A signature-estimand module builds target-specific participant maps using the existing Level 2
   nuisance resolver.
4. A sensor-cluster module validates complete tensors, constructs adjacency, performs synchronized
   permutation inference, and returns immutable result data plus audit frames.
5. A shared renderer draws already-computed maps and corrected sensor masks without performing
   analysis.
6. Two thin standalone writer modules stage and atomically publish their output families.

Analysis code does not depend on Matplotlib. Rendering code does not read raw artifacts or compute
statistics.

## Output families

Each standalone writer publishes:

- editable SVG;
- 600-DPI PNG;
- participant-by-row-by-band-by-sensor effect TSV and Parquet;
- sensor-level cohort effect, t-statistic, and corrected-cluster membership TSV and Parquet;
- cluster-level TSV containing map, sign, sensors, extent, mass, and corrected p-value;
- family-level inference TSV containing population, thresholds, null count, seed, and correction
  metadata;
- complete caption text;
- JSON provenance manifest.

The construct family additionally writes its configured Fp1/Fp2 channel-sensitivity tables. The
manifest records configuration values, resolved source paths and SHA-256 checksums, participant and
channel order, montage and adjacency metadata, permutation method, requested and actual draw counts,
software versions, output checksums, and exclusion reasons.

All files are created in a staging directory, validated, and then promoted together. A failed run
cannot leave a partially updated publication family.

## Error handling

The commands fail before publication for:

- missing source artifacts or nuisance columns;
- malformed feature names or duplicate trials;
- unexpected bands, temperatures, targets, channels, or target-specific nuisance definitions;
- non-finite or nonpositive power inputs;
- trial alignment disagreement;
- too few estimable participants after audited participant exclusions;
- missing, duplicated, or incompatible sensor positions;
- invalid or disconnected adjacency;
- invalid permutation settings or insufficient sign-flip resolution;
- output schema or atomic-promotion failures.

No fallback, data imputation, silent channel removal, alternative correction, or partial publication
is permitted.

## Testing

Tests use compact synthetic Study 1 artifacts and cover:

- exact channel-level dB reconstruction and feature-name parsing;
- trial, band, channel, target, and montage validation;
- hand-calculated temperature slopes;
- hand-calculated partial correlations for intensity and NPS;
- mandatory NPS adjustment for SIIPS1;
- primary and complementary construct channel scopes;
- confirmatory signature channel exclusion;
- complete-family participant selection and exclusion audits;
- positive and negative adjacency clusters;
- synchronized sign flips across rows and bands;
- maximum-cluster correction across all ten maps;
- exact and sampled permutation paths with deterministic results;
- corrected p-values against small enumerable reference cases;
- fixed layout, map order, shared color limits, and corrected sensor rings;
- absence of overlays when no cluster survives;
- SVG, PNG, table, caption, and manifest schemas;
- atomic promotion and rollback on failure;
- CLI generation from synthetic prepared-target and prepared-feature roots.

Relevant focused tests run before the broader Study 1 pipeline and maintainability gates.
